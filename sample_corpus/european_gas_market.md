# European natural gas market notes

European gas storage can be high while prompt and forward prices rise because inventories are only one part of the balance. Prices also respond to the expected rate of withdrawals, pipeline supply, LNG competition, weather forecasts, and risk premiums. High storage is a buffer, not a guarantee of low marginal prices.

Norwegian maintenance or outages can reduce pipeline nominations into Northwest Europe. Because Norway is a major flexible supplier, even temporary disruption can lift the price of replacing marginal molecules. At the same time, stronger Asian LNG demand can redirect flexible cargoes away from Europe and raise the delivered replacement cost.

Weather expectations affect both residential demand and the power sector. Colder forecasts increase expected heating demand, while weak wind generation can raise gas-fired power demand. Conversely, mild weather, strong renewable output, and above-normal inventories can limit the duration of a rally.

Storage percentages should be interpreted alongside absolute working gas, withdrawal capability, seasonal targets, and the expected balance through the end of winter. A high headline percentage may coexist with tighter near-term flows or concern about the next refill season.
